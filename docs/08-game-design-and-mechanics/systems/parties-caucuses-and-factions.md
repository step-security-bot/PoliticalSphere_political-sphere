## Overview

Political parties, caucuses, and factions are central to Political Sphere's parliamentary gameplay. While players can operate as independents, party membership provides significant strategic advantages. The system balances ideological flexibility with reputation consequences for inconsistency.

---

## Core Principles

- **Optional but Beneficial:** Party membership is not mandatory but provides substantial advantages
- **Flexible Creation:** Players can form new parties after meeting requirements
- **Ideological Freedom:** No hard constraints on policy positions
- **Soft Enforcement:** Reputation and media pressure guide party consistency
- **Dynamic Politics:** Parties can evolve, merge, split, or dissolve
- **Real-World Parallels:** Party dynamics mirror real parliamentary systems

---

## Party Membership

### Independent MPs

**Capabilities:**
- Full parliamentary rights
- Speak, vote, and propose legislation
- Join committees
- Stand for election

**Limitations:**
- No party resources (funding, staff, research)
- Limited coordination with other MPs
- Reduced media attention
- Harder to advance to ministerial positions
- No automatic speaking slots
- Must build every alliance individually

**Strategic Niche:**
- Maximum flexibility in voting
- Appeal to voters seeking independence
- Can be "kingmaker" in tight votes
- Build cross-party reputation

---

### Party Members

**Benefits:**
- **Shared Resources:** Party budget, research staff, campaign infrastructure
- **Coordination:** Whip system for organized voting
- **Media Platform:** Party amplifies member messages
- **Electoral Support:** Party brand, funding, and campaigning
- **Career Advancement:** Path to ministerial and leadership roles
- **Speaking Priority:** Party positions get guaranteed speaking slots
- **Policy Development:** Collaborative platform creation

**Obligations:**
- Generally vote with party (not mechanically enforced)
- Contribute to party resources
- Represent party values (loosely)
- Support party candidates in elections
- Attend party meetings and caucuses

**Leaving Consequences:**
- Lose access to party resources immediately
- Media scrutiny on reasons for departure
- Reputation impact (depends on circumstances)
- Electoral vulnerability if party withdraws support

## Party Formation

### Creation Requirements

To establish a new party, players must:

1. **Founding Members:** Minimum 3 MPs (or equivalent reputation from other roles)
2. **Registration:** Submit party name, core values, and initial platform
3. **Reputation Threshold:** Combined founder reputation ≥ 150
4. **Registration Fee:** Influence points cost (prevents frivolous parties)
5. **Constitutional Compliance:** Platform must not violate game rules (neutrality, ethics)

### Party Constitution

New parties must define:

- **Party Name:** Unique identifier
- **Core Values:** 3-5 fundamental principles
- **Leadership Structure:** How leaders are chosen
- **Decision-Making:** Policy development process
- **Membership Rules:** Entry and exit procedures
- **Discipline Approach:** How party handles rebellions

## Core Values and Ideological Drift

### Soft Ideology System

**No Hard Constraints:**
- Parties are not locked to rigid positions
- Members can vote against party line
- Policies can evolve over time

**Reputation Pressure:**
When parties deviate significantly from core values:

1. **Media Scrutiny:** Journalists highlight inconsistencies
2. **Reputation Impact:** Party and members lose credibility
3. **Voter Confusion:** Electoral support may decline
4. **Internal Tension:** AI NPCs may express concern or dissent
5. **Factional Risks:** May trigger internal splits

**Examples of Drift:**
- Economic party abandoning fiscal promises
- Civil liberties party supporting surveillance
- Anti-corruption party involved in scandals
- Environmental party backing polluting industries

### Positive Evolution vs. Contradiction

**Acceptable Evolution:**
- Adapting policies to new evidence
- Modernizing positions while keeping values
- Forming pragmatic coalitions
- Compromise on secondary issues

**Problematic Contradiction:**
- Complete reversal of founding principles
- Supporting policies directly opposing core values
- Systematic hypocrisy between words and votes
- Abandoning identity for short-term gain

## Party Resources

### Financial Resources

**Income Sources:**
- Member contributions (automated %)
- Electoral funding (proportional to votes)
- Fundraising events (player-organized)
- Donations (transparent, with limits)

**Expenditures:**
- Campaign costs
- Staff salaries
- Research and policy development
- Media and advertising
- Constituency offices
- Whip operation

**Bankruptcy:**
- Parties can run out of funds
- Limits campaign effectiveness
- May force mergers or dissolution

---

### Influence and Political Capital

**Party Influence Pool:**
- Shared resource for strategic actions
- Spend on:
  - Amending bills
  - Calling votes
  - Requesting debates
  - Emergency business
  - Media campaigns

**Earning Influence:**
- Electoral victories
- Successful legislation
- Media presence
- Effective governance
- Cross-party achievements

---

## Party Leadership

### Leadership Roles

**Party Leader:**
- Sets strategic direction
- Public face of party
- Candidate for Prime Minister (if largest party)
- Final say on major decisions

**Deputy Leader:**
- Supports leader
- Steps in when leader absent
- Specific portfolio responsibilities

**Chief Whip:**
- Coordinates voting strategy
- Manages party discipline (through persuasion, not force)
- Negotiates with other parties
- Tracks member positions

**Policy Coordinators:**
- Develop party positions on specific areas
- Brief members on legislation
- Draft party bills and amendments

---

### Leadership Selection

**Election Process:**
- Internal party election
- Members vote for leader
- Frequency defined in party constitution (typically yearly or after electoral defeat)

**Challenges:**
- Members can challenge sitting leader
- Requires threshold of support (typically 20-30% of members)
- Triggers leadership election

**Removal:**
- Vote of no confidence from party members
- Automatic trigger after electoral collapse
- Resignation (voluntary)

---

## Whip System

### Party Discipline (Soft)

**Three-Line Whip (Strong):**
- Party strongly requests member support
- Tied to confidence, budget, or key manifesto items
- Rebelling creates media attention
- Reputation impact for defiance
- May affect future candidate selection

**Two-Line Whip (Moderate):**
- Party prefers member support
- Not critical to government/party survival
- Conscience votes acceptable with explanation

**One-Line Whip (Weak):**
- Party has position but little pressure
- Free vote in practice
- Opportunity to represent constituents over party

**No Enforcement Mechanism:**
- Whips cannot mechanically force votes
- Rely on persuasion, resources, and reputation
- Persistent rebellion has natural consequences (media, selection, advancement)

## Caucuses and Factions

### Intra-Party Groups

**Caucuses:**
- Organized sub-groups within parties
- Share specific interests or ideologies
- Coordinate on particular issues
- Examples: Rural caucus, progressive caucus, security caucus

**Formation:**
- Minimum 2 members
- Register with party leadership (optional)
- No resource pool (informal)

**Purpose:**
- Amplify specific perspectives
- Negotiate with leadership
- Develop specialized policy
- Build cross-party alliances on narrow issues

---

### Cross-Party Groups

**All-Party Parliamentary Groups (APPGs):**
- MPs from multiple parties
- Focus on specific topics or regions
- No formal power but significant influence
- Examples: APPG on Education, APPG on Climate, APPG on Trade

**Coalition Building:**
- Informal alliances for specific votes
- Confidence and supply agreements
- Full coalition governments
- Issue-by-issue cooperation

---

## Party Types and Archetypes

### Major Parties

- Large membership base
- Significant resources
- Government or opposition status
- Full media coverage
- Established infrastructure

### Minor Parties

- Smaller membership
- Limited resources
- Balance-of-power potential in hung parliaments
- Focused platform
- Can punch above weight on specific issues

### Single-Issue Parties

- Narrow focus (environment, reform, regional)
- Attract passionate supporters
- Risk of irrelevance if issue resolved
- Can force major parties to adopt positions

### Regional Parties

- Represent specific constituencies or regions
- Strong local support
- Limited national appeal
- Kingmakers in divided parliaments

---

## Party Dynamics

### Mergers

**Requirements:**
- Both party leaderships agree
- Member referendum in each party (60% threshold)
- Asset combination
- Name and identity negotiation

**Process:**
- Declare intent
- Negotiate terms
- Member votes
- Registration of new entity
- Integration period

---

### Splits

**Triggers:**
- Irreconcilable ideological differences
- Leadership crises
- Major policy reversals
- Scandal or ethical breach

**Process:**
- Faction declares independence
- Members choose sides
- Asset division (proportional to membership)
- New party registration (if needed)
- Media coverage and electoral impact

---

### Dissolution

**Voluntary:**
- Party votes to dissolve
- Assets distributed or donated
- Members become independents or join other parties

**Forced:**
- Constitutional violation
- Sustained rule-breaking
- No active members remaining
- Bankruptcy without recovery plan

---

## Electoral Impact

### Party Brand

- Reputation affects all party candidates
- Strong brand boosts marginal candidates
- Weak brand creates liability
- Scandals hurt entire party

### Swing Dynamics

- Parties rise and fall based on performance
- Electoral swings reflect public opinion
- Hung parliaments create strategic opportunities
- Landslides consolidate power

### Candidate Selection

- Parties select candidates for constituencies
- Internal elections or leadership appointments
- Sitting MPs typically re-selected
- Rebellious MPs may face challenges

---

## AI NPC Party Members

**Parliamentary AI NPCs:**
- Members of parties (player-created or AI-generated)
- Vote generally with party line
- Can rebel based on AI personality and circumstances
- Contribute to party dynamics and debates
- Represent constituencies
- Stand for re-election

**Leadership:**
- Can serve as party leaders
- Human players prioritized for major party leadership
- AI leaders have consistent but adaptable strategies

**Dynamic Positions:**
- AI positions evolve naturally
- Respond to events, debates, and public opinion
- Can change parties (rarely)
- Reflect realistic political fluidity

## Integration with Other Systems

### Elections

- Party performance in elections determines seats
- Coalition-building after elections
- Confidence and supply agreements

### Media

- Party press offices manage communications
- Leadership interviews and appearances
- Media scrutiny of party promises vs. actions

### Reputation

- Party reputation distinct from individual reputation
- Both affect electoral outcomes
- Scandals can hurt both

### Resources

- Party budgets fund campaigns and operations
- Resource management critical to success
